A note for parents/ The Athlete Check/ College readiness

Send them ready.

Call Kenny directly → See the College Readiness Score

The first conversation is a conversation, not a pitch.

You have spent eighteen years getting your child to this point. The applications, the early mornings, the tuition conversations, the hard ones. Here is what nobody tells you: the acceptance letter proves they can do the work. It does not prove they are ready to live it.

In under a year they will be somewhere else, with nobody making them go to anything. No study hall, no ride home, no one noticing when they stop showing up. That is the part no transcript measures and no campus tour explains.

What follows is who I am, exactly what I do with your son or daughter, what they walk away holding, and what it costs.

01 — Why I built this

I looked unstoppable. I was falling apart.

I graduated from The Citadel in 1997 and walked straight into cybersecurity during one of the most explosive stretches in tech history. By 24 I was a millionaire. For the next 27 years I chased the next promotion, the next quota, and every other external marker corporate America hands you as proof that you matter.

The higher I climbed, the less I recognised myself. I got very good at wearing what I now call the Superman cape — composed, capable, crushing it in every meeting, while privately dealing with depression, burnout, and a body that was breaking down. I believed asking for help was the one thing a leader could never do. Eventually the pressure caught up with me and I lost nearly everything.

What I found underneath the burnout was the real problem: I had lost my identity. Rebuilding took what Ironman racing takes — a plan, daily reps, and people who would not let me quit. I have since finished ten Ironman races and beaten Stage 3 prostate cancer. I earned my MBA from The Citadel’s Baker School of Business, and I am completing my Master’s in Clinical Mental Health Counseling at Liberty University, because I wanted real clinical training behind the lived experience before I ever coached anyone through theirs.

Nobody reached me at eighteen. That is the entire reason this exists.

02 — What I actually do with your child

Four steps. No mystery about any of them.

This is the part most programmes keep vague. I would rather you read it in full before we ever speak, so the first call is about your child and not about what the thing is.

Step 01

I get to know them

Before anything is measured, we talk. A call with you first, then a call with your son or daughter on their own. I want to hear how they describe their own life — what they think they are good at, what they are avoiding, what they actually want from the next four years as opposed to what they have been told to want.

No money changes hands until I am confident this is a fit. If it is not, I will tell you that on the first call and we part well.

Step 02

The Athlete Check

They take the College Readiness Score — forty questions across eight domains, scored out of 1,000, with a level on the ladder and a breakdown of every domain.

This is the honest baseline. It is not a personality quiz and it is not designed to flatter anyone. It tells us where the strength actually sits and which two or three things are worth the next ninety days.

Step 03

The system

Three months of work built on the score, not on a generic curriculum. Your young person gets both halves of how I coach:

  • Direct one-to-one time with me — not a course, not a portal, not a chatbot with my face on it.
  • A small cohort of other students doing the same hard work at the same time. At this age, the peer group does as much of the work as I do.
  • A 90-day plan built around the domains that move fastest from where they are.
  • The five pillars — discipline, resilience, consistency, accountability, and community.

Step 04

The sign-off

At the end, we sit down — you, them, and me — and I give you my honest read. If they have done the work and they are ready, I certify it in writing.

If they are not there yet, I will say so plainly and tell you what is still missing. A certification I hand out regardless of the work is worth nothing to anybody.

03 — What they walk away holding

A certification with my name on it.

Certified College Ready

Issued by IronMind Advisors · The Athlete Check

A written certification stating that your son or daughter has completed the College Readiness Score, done the ninety days of work, and reached a standard I am willing to put my name against. It travels with them — something to hand a coach, an admissions officer, or a first employer alongside a transcript that only says what they know.

Let me be straight about what this is. It is not accredited by a university, an athletic association, or a licensing board, and I will never imply otherwise. It carries exactly as much weight as my name carries with the person reading it — which is precisely why I will not sign one that is not true.

Parent to parent

Three things you should know before you call.

I will hold them to a standard

I will not co-sign excuses. If your son or daughter brings me complaints disconnected from growth, I will redirect them. That is not a lack of care — it is the opposite, and it is the whole reason this works.

This is coaching, not therapy

I am completing clinical training and I am not your child’s counselor. What I do is mentorship and coaching. If something surfaces that belongs with a licensed clinician, I will tell you plainly and help you find the right person — I will not quietly work around it.

You are not on the outside of this

We start with a call with you and we finish with a sit-down that includes you. Your young person needs room to be honest with me, and you need to know how it is going. Those two things are not in conflict, and I will tell you at the start exactly how I balance them.
