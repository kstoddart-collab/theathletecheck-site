04

The 12-Point Accountability Rubric

Every after-action review is scored across four competency pillars. This is the same language your staff can use on the sideline — and it is what makes the program measurable rather than motivational.
Competency Pillar Level 1 — Developing · 1 pt Level 2 — Proficient · 2 pts Level 3 — IronMind Standard · 3 pts
1. Self-Awareness & Candor Vague, surface-level responses; defensive justifications; denies personal errors. Identifies mistakes accurately, but hesitates to examine the root internal self-talk. Radically honest and fully objective; transcribes internal mental state without excuses.
2. Reset Execution & Recovery Speed Failed to trigger the reset; frustration compounded and ruined multiple plays or quarters. Reset deployed after a delay; took several possessions to regain full composure. Reset deployed instantly; back to baseline neutral focus in under 3 seconds or by the next play.
3. Locus of Control Blames external factors — referees, weather, coaching staff, teammates' mistakes. Accepts responsibility, but the adjustment is generic: "try harder," "be more focused." Zero external blaming; specifies a precise, controllable behavioral adjustment for the next practice.
4. Sideline Presence & Leadership Slumped body language, isolation, visible anger, or negative vocal tone. Neutral presence; did not drain team energy, but failed to actively elevate others. Proactive vocal leadership; contagious positive energy; actively stabilized teammates during deficits.
Certification threshold: four consecutive game-day AARs averaging ≥ 9 of 12. Scores below that don't fail an athlete — they set the next phase of work.
